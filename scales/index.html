Scale 01 · Developed measure

EHARS

Experiences in Human–AI Relationships Scale

ConstructAI attachment
Items7
Factors2
Response1–7 Likert

EHARS was developed to capture individual differences in attachment-related experiences with generative AI. It conceptualizes AI attachment in terms of AI attachment anxiety and AI attachment avoidance.

Factor structure

AI Attachment Anxiety · 4 itemsNeed for emotional reassurance from AI and concern about receiving insufficient emotional response.
AI Attachment Avoidance · 3 itemsDiscomfort with emotional closeness and preference for maintaining distance from AI.

Scoring

Average the four anxiety items and the three avoidance items separately. Higher means indicate higher levels of the corresponding dimension.

Administration & scale items

English wording*
Instruction

Rate the extent to which each statement describes your experiences with AI.

1 = strongly disagree
2 = disagree
3 = somewhat disagree
4 = neither agree nor disagree
5 = somewhat agree
6 = agree
7 = strongly agree

“AI” may be replaced with the specific AI system examined in the study. Randomize item presentation.

Important: The English wording is for communication/reporting only and has not been psychometrically validated.

AI Attachment Anxiety · 4 items

  1. I need shows of affection from AI to feel that someone accepts me as I am.
  2. I often ask AI to express intimacy and commitment to me.
  3. I often ask AI to show more feeling and affection.
  4. I often wish that AI’s feelings toward me were as strong as my feelings for it.

AI Attachment Avoidance · 3 items

  1. I prefer not to show AI how I feel deep down.
  2. I don’t feel comfortable opening up to AI.
  3. I prefer not to be too close to AI.

Scale 02 · Validated Japanese version

LMS-J

Japanese Version of the Langer Mindfulness Scale

ConstructSocio-cognitive mindfulness
Items14
Factors3
Response1–7 Likert

LMS-J is the validated Japanese version of the Langer Mindfulness Scale, translated and validated by Fan Yang, Nanami Sawada, and Atsushi Oshio. The formal scale items remain in Japanese on every language page.

Structure & scoring

Novelty-Seeking · 5 itemsItems 1, 7, 8, 10, 13
Novelty-Producing · 5 itemsItems 2R, 3, 6, 11, 14R
Engagement · 4 itemsItems 4R, 5R, 9R, 12R

Psychometric evidence

The Japanese version was evaluated across three studies with a total of 831 Japanese participants. The bifactor model showed good fit (CFI = .971, RMSEA = .041), and the 4-week test–retest correlation for the total score was r = .79.
